Building this portfolio was my first time ever writing HTML or CSS, so the biggest
challenge was learning to think in structure instead of appearance. I kept wanting to make
things look a certain way before I understood how the page was organized underneath.
Learning when to use a heading, a figure, or a list, and how Flexbox and Grid decide where
things sit, took patience and a lot of trial and error.
What I’m most proud of is the content. As an English major, I wanted the site to sound
like a person, not a template. The “Welcome In!” greeting, the honest note that my projects are
works in progress, and the captioned photo from my study abroad in Hokkaido give visitors a
sense of who I am. Meanwhile, the clean layout, clear navigation, and organized Skills, Projects,
and Contact sections keep it moderately professional. Balancing those two goals was the part I
enjoyed most.
Accessibility shaped my decisions from the start. I used semantic HTML so the page has
a logical outline, wrote descriptive alt text for my images, added captions to figures, and made
link text meaningful instead of using “click here.” A consistent navigation bar helps visitors move
around the site easily.
With more time, I would expand the Projects section with short write-ups of what I
learned from each one. I would also run the site through an accessibility checker, test it using
only a keyboard, and refine the color contrast and mobile layout. I would proofread more
carefully too, since small typos slip through when you are focused on code. Most of all, I want
this site to keep growing as I add new projects.
